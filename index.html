01 · Flagship · M.Sc. thesis 2025

A shoe insole that measures gait using the energy of each footstep

Intelligent, energy-aware biomedical wearable for continuous gait analysis in rehabilitation. IMTEK Laboratory for the Design of Microsystems & livMatS, University of Freiburg.

Problem

Gait is measured in labs, not in life

About 2.4 billion people have a condition that could benefit from rehabilitation, yet gait analysis is a short lab snapshot. How patients actually walk at home goes unmeasured.

Approach

Self-powered sensing + edge AI

A triboelectric nanogenerator (TENG) in the heel, a bespoke electrometer front-end for its GΩ-impedance output, an ESP32 and a frequency-domain XGBoost classifier.

Result

93.58% across four activities

An end-to-end validated proof of concept, with the TENG demonstrated as both sensor and energy source. The paper is under review at IEEE Sensors Journal.

A self-powered transducer

A contact-separation TENG: when the heel strikes, copper and PDMS touch and exchange charge; as they separate, the changing gap drives current. Each footstep generates its own signal.

  • 2 × 2 cm active area in the heel's peak-force zone
  • PDMS dielectric, copper electrodes, 1 mm air gap
  • Layer count optimised from 3 to 6 for amplitude
  • ≈ 800 mV open-circuit, 10.58 µW peak power during gait

Solving the impedance problem

Connected straight to an ADC, a GΩ-impedance source would be loaded and its signal destroyed. The three-stage chain preserves the waveform and maps ±200 mV into 0–3.3 V.

  • AD795 electrometer, ≤ 0.3 pA bias: under 50 µV error across 10 MΩ
  • AC coupling + 1.65 V mid-rail bias so neither half-wave clips
  • TLV2772 low-impedance ADC driver on a 3.3 V LDO rail

Custom acquisition board

A two-layer PCB of about 60 × 40 mm feeds the ESP32's 12-bit ADC and streams data wirelessly.

  • Short TENG input trace to minimise parasitic capacitance
  • Split ±9 V / 3.3 V domains with a single-point analog/digital ground
  • Decoupling within 5 mm of IC power pins
  • Modular connectors: sensor, batteries and ADC out

From waveform to activity label

TENG waveforms are quasi-periodic and sensitive to contact mechanics, so I moved the problem into the frequency domain, where each activity has its own spectral signature.

  • Sliding-window FFT, 0.2 s hop (99.8 % overlap)
  • Dominant frequency, spectral energy and centroid + z-scored height and weight
  • XGBoost: 1000 trees, depth 6, softprob outputs
  • Trees over LSTM: minutes to train, explainable, MCU-friendly

Critical review

What I would tighten

  • Subject-level validation: the 75/25 split was made at window level. Leave-one-subject-out is the honest next benchmark.
  • Identity vs activity: height and weight carry about half the importance, so generalisation to unseen bodies needs checking.
  • Scale: 12 healthy adults in a lab. Patients and in-the-wild data come next.
Roadmap

Where it goes next

  • Battery-less: on-board harvesting and power management.
  • Sensor fusion: TENG + low-power IMU for gait-phase and quality-of-movement metrics.
  • On-device inference on the ESP32 for real-time feedback to patients and therapists.
